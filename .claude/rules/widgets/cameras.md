# Cameras widget (`cameras`)

- Snapshot tiles (`devices` filtered to `class: camera|doorbell`), 2 per row at 16:9; a lone tile spans the row, and so does one of an odd number: the last, or with `layout` (wk-052, a user's request for one large camera over two small ones) `top` the first, `single` every one (`spansRow()`; `bottom`, the default, for a missing or unknown value). `lib/CameraService.ts`; transparent; the native 10 px radius and the name on a dark gradient (14/20 bold white).
- **Camera 1 … 6** (`camera1`…`camera6`, autocompletes, last; 1.2.0, a user's report: Synology Surveillance Station's cameras are class `sensor`, so the `devices` picker, which Homey filters by class and capabilities only, never listed them, and the iOS personal Home screen keeps its dashboard on the phone, so ids can't be put in through the API). `CameraService.listCameras()` lists None, then class camera or doorbell or `hasCameraMedia()` (a `camera` image or a video), any class; a snapshot-only camera (a Doorbird) counts, as the widget shows snapshots, not only streams (Homey's own camera dashboard lists only devices with video). The mount passes the picks to `wireDeviceState()` as `more`: after the `devices` ids, a repeat and `none` left out, so a widget without them is as before. `npm run test-dashboards -- cameras` has *Camera slots only* and one with the picked device again in Camera 1. Considered and dropped: adding `sensor` to the filter (the phone's picker then lists every sensor; never released).
- `refresh` (5/10/30/60 s, default 10) is the snapshot interval; `/state` itself refreshes every 5 min. Snapshots load one camera at a time and swap in once decoded; one that takes over 20 s is skipped that round (a hanging camera held up the rest); polling stops while `document.hidden`. A snapshot older than 2 intervals + 5 s shows its time (two-digit hours; 24 or 12 by the app's Time format (CLAUDE.md *Time format*; until 1.3.0 a `timeFormat` setting of its own, the Clock's, wk-085); the mount's `wireDeviceState(…, {times: true})` reloads when it changes).
- Endpoints: `GET /state?deviceIds=` (`{id, name, icon, image: {id, url, lastUpdated} | null, video, videos, h265}`; `video` is `videos[0]`), `GET /snapshot?deviceId=` (`{type, data}` base64), `POST /video/offer {videoId, offer}` (→ `{answer, streamId}`), `POST /video/keepalive {videoId, streamId}` and `POST /report {text, warn}` (the widget's findings; with `warn`, e.g. a stream that wouldn't play or a failed grab or keep-alive, always logged, so a user's diagnostics show it; the rest at debug).
- Snapshots: the widget loads `image.url` (`/api/image/<id>?t=…`) itself; a camera whose direct load fails switches to `/snapshot` for the session (that camera only) and reports it. `/snapshot` shares one fetch per image for 2 s and keeps nothing longer (snapshots can be 2 MB). The service's per-camera memory (the last entry, kept when Homey is only busy, and the image URL) is dropped once no request has asked for that camera in 10 min. On the LAN the phone app loads the frame from `http://<Homey IP>` and direct loads work (2026-10-04); away from home (through Homey's cloud) isn't verified: check the `Cameras widget:` debug lines.
- Live view: a tap on a tile with `video` creates a `recvonly` video `RTCPeerConnection`, waits for ICE gathering (≤ 2 s; Homey's answer has all its candidates, no trickle), posts the offer and plays a muted `<video>` with a red LIVE chip. The click-after-touch guard uses `Date.now()`, not `opts.now`, so a fixed clock doesn't swallow clicks. The live tile (`.expanded`) covers the whole grid, letterboxed; a single row first grows to a full-width 16:9 (`min-height`, measured before the tile leaves the grid, reported through `onHeight`). One at a time; a tap, the `liveFor` limit, hiding the frame or 15 s without a frame ends it (`framesDecoded` from `getStats()` every 5 s). Keep-alive every 10 s (a failure reported once). While a camera is live (connecting too), `refresh()` loads no snapshots: the other tiles are hidden, and `stopLive()` refreshes them. Verified on the LAN from a desktop browser and the phone app (2026-10-04); not away from home, where only Homey's public srflx candidates could connect.
- The player is the kit's `playCameraStream()`, shared with the popups' live video (`.claude/rules/widgets/popups.md`): the stream walk, the timeouts, the keep-alive, the frame check and telling away from home (`onEnded`'s `away`). `startLive()` only adds what's this widget's: `videoOrder()`, the reports, and the tile's messages (a popup falls back to snapshots). Below, `startLive()`'s stream walk is the player's.
- How long (wk-053, a user wanted live view to stay on): `liveFor` ("Live view stays on", after `refresh`; `30`, `60`, `300` (the default) or `tap`, "Until tapped"; `liveLimit()` in `widget.js`: a missing or unknown value is 5 min). Its hint carries the "Tap a camera for live video" that was `refresh`'s. Until tapped there's no timer, and a live view that `pause()` ended (the page hidden, or the stack's `setVisible(false)`) comes back: `pause()` keeps the camera in `resumeId` (a second `pause()` keeps it), and `mount.js`'s `startSnapshots()` calls `widget.resume()` before refreshing. A tap clears it, and a camera that went `missing` or left the list isn't resumed. A stream that drops still ends with *Live view failed* (the user's choice, no reconnect). Not yet tried: a stream longer than 5 min through Homey's go2rtc, and whether the phone apps' WebViews fire `visibilitychange` when the screen turns off (if they only freeze the page, the frame check ends it on wake). `npm run test-dashboards -- cameras` has *Live until tapped* and *Live 30 s*.
- **Always live** (`autoLive`, an autocomplete after `liveFor`, `listCameras()`; 1.3.0, the user's ask, 2026-11-07, from the same community question): that camera goes live by itself when the page shows (`resume()`, and `setState()` for a first read that was busy), fills the widget as a tap would, and has no `liveFor` limit. The mount adds it to `more`, so it needn't be among the picked ones; with `liveView` off it does nothing. A tap ends it and the grid shows: it comes back after 60 s (`AUTO_BACK_MS`), the same after a tapped camera ends or a try where no stream played. A stream that played and was lost comes back after 5 s (`AUTO_LOST_MS`), the widget's only reconnect; a tapped camera still doesn't. Away from home or H.265 only, another try can't help, so it stays on snapshots until the page next shows (`autoGaveUp`). `pause()` stops it all. It's one camera on purpose: `live` is one connection, and a grid of streams is untested on go2rtc. Tested with fake connections in `test/cameras-widget.test.ts`, the path it shares with a tap verified on the LAN (2026-10-04); not yet on a real camera. `npm run test-dashboards -- cameras` has *Always live*.
- Live view off (the user's ask, 2026-11-01): `liveView` ("Live view", a checkbox before `liveFor`, on; a missing value is on): off, a tap does nothing (`press()` and `resume()` return, no `has-video` pointer cursor), so no tile expands and no live stream opens. Video-only cameras still get their frame grabs (a short stream each), or they'd show nothing. `npm run test-dashboards -- cameras` has *Live view off*.
- Video-only cameras (a user's Aqaras: `images: []`, one `main` RTSP video, 2026-10-12): `grabFrame()` opens a short-lived stream (`openStream()`, shared with live view, no keep-alive) into the tile's hidden `<video>`, draws its first frame to a canvas (≤ 640 px wide, JPEG) and closes it. At most every max(60 s, `refresh`) per camera, 5 min after a failure (reported once: `frame grab of <name> failed: …`), one stream at a time, never while a camera is live; a tap or `pause()` cancels it. Ending live view keeps the last frame. "No snapshot" shows only without video, or after a failed grab with no frame yet. Not yet tried with a real Aqara.
- Stream choice (wk-040: a user's 4K Reolink showed only "Live view failed"): `cameraVideos()` ranks `sub` (by id or title), then titles that say H.264, then those that don't say H.265/HEVC, H.265 last; `h265Videos()` lists the H.265 ones. **Homey's go2rtc answers H.264 only**: an H.265 offer is refused at once (`500 WebRTC offer failed: … codecs not matched: video:H265, audio:AAC => video:VP8, video:VP9, video:H264, video:AV1`, 2026-10-15). `startLive()` tries them in turn (`videoOrder()`: the one that last played first, those that failed before a frame last), each on a fresh connection, the tile staying expanded; a refused offer, a failed connection or no frame in 10 s (15 s for the last) moves on, each reported (`stream 1 of 2 failed: …`). On the Homey, `main` refused then `sub` playing took 4.6 s. Frame grabs use the same order. None played: `Live view failed`, or `cameras.liveH265` ("H.265 can't play live. Try H.264.") when every stream is H.265 (a Reolink on a Home Hub or NVR has one stream; the Reolink app is closed source, issues at github.com/koktail-nl/com.reolink.issues); a video-only camera with only H.265 says the same as its empty text. The note is up to 2 lines for 6 s.
- Away from home (a user's report, 2026-11-03: live view only on the LAN): the offer goes through Homey's cloud, but the media is WebRTC straight to go2rtc (host candidates on 8555, public srflx ones the router blocks; no TURN), so the connection never comes up. `startLive()` counts streams whose offer was answered but whose `connectionState` never reached `connected` (`unreached`, `reached`); none played and at least one such: `cameras.liveAway` ("Live view needs your home network or a VPN."; H.265 still wins) and the report adds `Homey answered, but its video server could not be reached (away from home?)`. README *Cameras* says a VPN works. Not tried: forwarding port 8555 (it would expose go2rtc), and how Homey's own camera view streams away from home (a relay the widget could use?).
